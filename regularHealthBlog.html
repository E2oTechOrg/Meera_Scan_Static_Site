Pap Smear Screening for Cervical Cancer Prevention

Menopause and Bone Density Test (DEXA) Why It Matters After 40

Osteoporosis is one of the most underdiagnosed conditions in Indian women. Because bone loss is painless and gradual, most women have no awareness of significant skeletal deterioration until a fracture — often of the hip, spine, or wrist — occurs. The menopause and bone density test (DEXA) is the clinical gold standard for measuring bone mineral density (BMD) and diagnosing osteoporosis or osteopenia before fracture risk becomes critical.

How the DEXA Bone Density Test Works

DEXA (Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry) uses two low-dose X-ray beams to measure the mineral content of bone at the lumbar spine and hip — the two sites most predictive of future fracture risk. The result is expressed as a T-score comparing the patient's BMD to the peak bone mass of a healthy young adult. The menopause and bone density test (DEXA) takes approximately 15–20 minutes, involves minimal radiation exposure, and requires no preparation or fasting.

T-Score Range Diagnosis Clinical Action
-1.0 and above Normal bone density Maintain calcium and vitamin D intake; repeat in 2–5 years
-1.0 to -2.5 Osteopenia (low bone mass) Dietary and lifestyle intervention; consider pharmacotherapy
-2.5 and below Osteoporosis Pharmacological treatment; fracture prevention strategy

The menopause and bone density test (DEXA) is particularly recommended for women who are post-menopausal, have a family history of osteoporotic fractures, have a low BMI, are on long-term corticosteroids, or have a history of amenorrhoea or premature menopause. Thyroid and Diabetes Screening for Women Over 40

Thyroid and diabetes screening for women forms an essential metabolic component of every preventive health checkup for women after 40. Both conditions share a profile of gradual, often silent onset, significant long-term health consequences if undetected, and excellent treatment outcomes when identified early through routine screening.

Thyroid Screening TSH and Free T4

Hypothyroidism — underactivity of the thyroid gland — affects a disproportionately high percentage of Indian women and its prevalence increases with age. Common presenting features including fatigue, unexplained weight gain, cold intolerance, hair thinning, constipation, and depressed mood are frequently attributed to normal ageing or stress, delaying diagnosis by months or years. A TSH (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone) blood test in the thyroid and diabetes screening for women panel detects both hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism reliably. Free T4 measurement is added when TSH is abnormal for complete assessment.

Diabetes and Pre-Diabetes Screening HbA1c and Fasting Glucose

Type 2 diabetes prevalence among Indian women above 40 is among the highest in the world, driven by genetic predisposition, sedentary lifestyle, and dietary patterns. The majority of cases are preceded by a years-long pre-diabetic phase during which blood glucose is elevated but below the diabetic threshold — a window in which lifestyle intervention can prevent or significantly delay progression to Type 2 diabetes. The thyroid and diabetes screening for women at Meera 4D Scans includes both fasting plasma glucose and HbA1c, the latter reflecting average blood glucose over the preceding 12 weeks and providing a more clinically reliable picture than a single fasting reading.
